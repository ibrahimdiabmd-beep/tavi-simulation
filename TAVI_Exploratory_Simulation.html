Layer A — Composite Access Determinants Score (static, structural)

Reproduces TAVI_Access_Score_Model.xlsx logic: min-max normalisation to 1–5 for the twelve measured indicators, equal 25% domain weights, aggregation within each domain by arithmetic mean over the indicators populated for that country, and across domains by geometric mean (bottleneck-sensitive, per Penchansky & Thomas's 1981 treatment of the five access dimensions as independently necessary — mirrors the UN HDI's 2010 switch to a geometric mean, Klugman, Rodriguez & Choi, 2011).
Two of the fourteen rows are ordinal judgements (eligibility basis; guideline currency) scored directly on the 1–5 scale rather than min-max normalised, and one German value ("no ceiling") is scored 5 by convention rather than measurement — all three are tagged accordingly in the tables below. Unlike the Layer B/C inputs, these rows do not carry hover tooltips; their provenance is given in the dissertation's Pillar reports.

Robustness checks (mirrors the Excel workbook's Composite_Score sheet)

Primary result above uses an equal-weight (25% each) geometric mean. The checks below test whether the UK-vs-Germany conclusion is an artefact of that specific choice.

Robustness check 3 — is the ranking an artefact of the weighting?

The equal-25% weighting is the single most obvious challenge to any composite index: change the weights, change the answer. This check settles the question exactly rather than by sampling. Because a weighted mean is a convex combination of its domain terms, the UK<Germany ordering can only be reversed by a re-weighting if the UK scores at or above Germany on at least one domain — so it is sufficient to check the sign of every domain gap, which covers all possible weightings at once, including extreme ones.

Layer B + C — Demand Funnel & Dynamic Waiting-List Engine

Core equations (Stickels et al., 2022): dW/dt = f − rT − rS − μW  →  tc = ln(1 + μW₀/Te) / μ    m(tc) = W₀ − Te·tc

How Te is calculated — reproduced from Stickels et al. (2022) Supplement 2 ("Data"): conversion is applied to the full post-capacity-increase SAVR pool, rS0·(1+capPct), so converted capacity compounds with newly-added capacity rather than being added as two independent terms:
Te = capPct·(rS0+rT0)  +  convPct·rS0·(1+capPct)·(ratio−1)   →   ×(1−ρ)
Strictly this is a bilinear form — linear in each lever separately, but carrying an interaction term (convPct·capPct·rS0) that an additive formulation omits. The model's genuine nonlinearity lies in the logarithm of the closed-form tc solution above, not in Te. This formula reproduces the Supplement's own worked numerical example — 20% capacity + 10% conversion at ratio 5:3 → Te = 3,232 procedures/yr — to within rounding. An earlier build of this tool omitted the interaction term and used 5:3 (an illustrative ratio from that worked example, and one of the paper's sensitivity ratios) as though it were the base case.

Geographic disaggregation (UK, 3-tier — calibrated to Aktaa et al., 2024)

Known internal inconsistency, disclosed. The three tier rates below (50 / 112 / 180 pmp) are illustrative representative values for the access bands reported by Aktaa et al. (2024). Their CCG-weighted mean is ≈109 pmp, which does not reproduce the confirmed national rate of 136 pmp used in Layer A — the bands are representative midpoints, not a partition that was constructed to reconcile to the national total. The tiers are therefore usable for relative comparison between tiers (which is what the inequity spread measures) but the absolute per-tier clearance times inherit this ≈20% discrepancy. They have deliberately not been rescaled to force agreement, since no published source gives the true within-band distribution.

Sensitivity analysis (UK) — which of the four pillars moves the outcome most?

One-at-a-time (OAT) tornado analysis: each parameter is swung across its literature-reported range (or a nominal ±15pp for pure levers, clamped to the source paper's 0–50% bounds — each bar states the range actually tested) while holding all others at the current UK slider settings, and the resulting change in tc (days) is recorded. Bars sorted by swing size.
Two limits govern how this may be read. First, OAT is local and holds everything else fixed, so it cannot detect interactions — including the capacity×conversion interaction that is explicitly built into Te. Second, the ranges are not commensurable: μ and W₀ are swung across the published 95% intervals of measured quantities, whereas the policy levers are swung by an arbitrary fixed ±15pp. The ordering therefore compares different kinds of quantity and is not a general ranking of the four determinant pillars; it indicates only local sensitivity around the settings currently on screen. Note also that μ and W₀ are epidemiological inputs, not levers: if either tops the ranking, that reflects uncertainty in the evidence base, not an intervention opportunity.

Optimisation — most cost-effective UK lever combinations

Brute-force grid search (not a black-box solver — chosen for transparency and defensibility in an MSc dissertation) over capacity increase (0–50%, step 10), conversion (0–50%, step 10) and funding-rigidity relaxation (0 → current ρ, step 0.1). Ranked by closeness to the Germany-scenario (tc, deaths) computed from the panel above — not a fixed budget or fixed mortality target, per the agreed design decision. Cost is shown alongside each option, not used as a filter.

Illustrative cost model: staffing = ⌈extra annual procedures ÷ 100 per-operator standard, Hildick-Smith et al. 2026⌉ × £161,600 fully-loaded consultant cost; + procedure cost = extra annual procedures × £26,145 (NHS FY2022 elective TAVI reference cost, via Blackman et al., 2024).
Three simplifications, stated explicitly. (1) The single TAVI tariff is applied to all added throughput, including added SAVR volume, so the mix effect is not costed. (2) Conversion is costed only on the net throughput it creates, not on the TAVI-minus-SAVR tariff differential for each converted case, which understates the true cost of a conversion-led strategy. (3) The staffing relationship is linear and unbounded — there are no capital costs, no catheterisation-laboratory or critical-care constraints, and no diminishing returns. Consequently these figures are an order-of-magnitude ranking device only. They are not a health-economic evaluation: there are no QALYs, no discounting and no time horizon, and they would not satisfy a NICE reference case.

Funding rigidity ρ as a scenario axis — removing the dependence on a single assumed value

ρ is the least directly evidenced parameter in this tool: no TAVI-specific evaluation of Blueteq's delay effect exists, so its default (0.275) is an illustrative midpoint, not a measurement. Any finding stated at a single assumed ρ is therefore partly a restatement of that assumption. This panel removes that dependence by sweeping ρ across its entire admissible range and reporting what holds throughout the range, rather than what happens at one chosen point. The intervention levers are held at the values currently set in the panel above.

Each row re-solves the same closed-form model with only ρ changed; ρ=0 is the friction-free reference (the state assumed by the original Stickels et al. model, which has no commissioning-friction concept).

Narrative Report — current scenario, in words

Generates a plain-language summary of exactly what you've set on this page right now, plus a waterfall table showing the marginal effect of each lever, added one at a time. Deterministic and rule-based (built from the same equations as the rest of the tool) — not an AI-written summary — so every sentence traces back to a number you can re-check above.
