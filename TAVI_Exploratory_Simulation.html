TAVI Exploratory Simulation — UK vs Germany

Supplementary Digital Tool for: Determinants of TAVI Utilisation: A Study of Operational Drivers in the UK Health System — Ibrahim Diab, MSc Healthcare Operational Management, WMG, University of Warwick.
This is an illustrative / exploratory tool, not a validated predictive model. It extends the published methodology of Stickels, C.P., Nadarajah, R., Gale, C.P. et al. (2022) 'Aortic stenosis post-COVID-19: a mathematical model on waiting lists and mortality', BMJ Open, 12, e059309, parameterised with the four determinant-pillar research passes underlying this dissertation. Every input is source-tagged (Confirmed Caveat Derived Assumption) — hover any label for its source.

Layer A — Composite Access Determinants Score (static, structural)

Reproduces TAVI_Access_Score_Model.xlsx logic: min-max normalisation to 1–5 per indicator, equal 25% domain weights, primary aggregation by geometric mean (bottleneck-sensitive, per Penchansky & Thomas's 1981 treatment of the five access dimensions as independently necessary — mirrors the UN HDI's 2010 switch to a geometric mean, Klugman, Rodriguez & Choi, 2011).

Robustness checks (mirrors the Excel workbook's Composite_Score sheet)

Primary result above uses an equal-weight (25% each) geometric mean. The checks below test whether the UK-vs-Germany conclusion is an artefact of that specific choice.

Adjustable weights (must sum to 100%) — Acceptability / Accessibility / Availability / Affordability

Layer B + C — Demand Funnel & Dynamic Waiting-List Engine

Core equations (Stickels et al., 2022): dW/dt = f − rT − rS − μW  →  tc = ln(1 + μW₀/Te) / μ    m(tc) = W₀ − Te·tc

Validation against the published paper — Te now reproduces Stickels et al.'s (2022) full nonlinear treatment, taken directly from their Supplement 2 ("Data") worked derivation: conversion is applied to the full post-capacity-increase SAVR pool, rS0·(1+capPct), so converted capacity compounds with newly-added capacity rather than being added as two independent terms, using their Methods-stated TAVI:SAVR time-equivalent ratio of 3:2 (corrected from an earlier build of this tool, which used the 5:3 ratio from the Supplement's illustrative worked example — one of the paper's own sensitivity-analysis ratios — rather than the ratio underlying its headline results). This formula reproduces the Supplement's own worked numerical example (20% capacity + 10% conversion → Te = 3,232 procedures/yr) to within rounding, and all four of the paper's reported scenarios now fall within its 95% CI, including capacity+20%/conversion+40% (headline, cited elsewhere in this dissertation) → 341.5 days / 793 deaths vs published 343 (281–410) / 784 (292–1324); 20%-capacity-only → 531.6 vs 536 (434–666); 5%-capacity-only → 1,342.1 vs 1,384 (1,025–1,994); and 50%-conversion-only → 660.1 vs 666 (533–848), which previously fell just outside the paper's CI (530.9 days) under this tool's earlier linear approximation. Treat this tool's absolute day/death counts as a close numerical reproduction, not a certified line-by-line re-implementation of the authors' own code (not published) — the underlying closed-form equations and this compounding logic are taken directly from their published Methods and Supplementary Material.

Sensitivity analysis (UK) — which of the four pillars moves the outcome most?

One-at-a-time (OAT) tornado analysis: each parameter is swung across its literature-reported range (or ±15pp for pure levers) while holding all others at the current UK slider settings, and the resulting change in tc (days) is recorded. Directly addresses the dissertation's core research question — which determinant pillar explains the most variation. Bars sorted by swing size (largest = most influential).

Narrative Report — current scenario, in words

Generates a plain-language summary of exactly what you've set on this page right now, plus a waterfall table showing the marginal effect of each lever, added one at a time. Deterministic and rule-based (built from the same equations as the rest of the tool) — not an AI-written summary — so every sentence traces back to a number you can re-check above.

Built as a supplementary digital tool, referenced in Chapter 5 and Appendix. All UK/Germany input defaults are drawn from the Pillar 1–4 Simulation Data Reports and TAVI_Access_Score_Model.xlsx prepared for this dissertation; hover any labelled field for its source and evidentiary status. Absolute outputs — especially any Germany-side figure — should be read as illustrative; relative, within-tool comparisons (UK vs Germany; before vs after a lever change) are more robust to the underlying evidence gaps than any single absolute number. Not for clinical or commissioning decision-making.
